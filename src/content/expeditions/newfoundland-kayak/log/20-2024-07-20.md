In the days following our last post (28-32) we reached and rounded Cape Norman, the northernmost point of our journey, with much difficulty. The conditions on the west coast of the Northern Peninsula were tough and we got to endure plenty of headwinds and RDF.

Day 28: We had rain and strong crosswinds for the majority of the day as we paddled from Port au Choix to the New Ferolle Peninsula. Once around New Ferolle we battled headwinds into the night and arrived in Plum Point 12km and 4 hours later. 65km

Day 29: The headwind persisted so we stayed put.

Day 30: We paddled from Plum Point to past Eddy’s Cove and entered the Strait of Belle Isle. Along the way, we encountered a set of standing waves caused by the Labrador Current and thousands of small fish we assumed to be Caplin surfacing in quick succession. Our route brought us within 20km of Labrador but it was too foggy to see across the strait. 65km

Day 31: A nasty NE wind picked up in our faces on an unprotected coastline so we reluctantly took another weather day. We took the free time to do some looking around and found some cool flowers and drinking puddles.

Day 32: The wind eased a bit and we took our window to move north. We stopped into the resettled community of Big Brook, picked up some more water and continued slowly toward Cape Norman. As we began to cross Pistolet bay, we saw lightning so hunkered down on Green Island until it passed. We eventually made it around Cape Onion into Ship Cove and thats when our luck started to turn. 60km
